# Representative celestial environment

The 47 named destinations retain their existing calculated positions and picking identities. All additional population dots are **representative procedural populations**, not identified or measured objects. Counts, brightness, particle sizes and selection of orbital elements serve readability, not a census or photometry. Stars are an unlabelled illustrative background, not a measured star catalogue.

## References verified 2026-10-06

- [JPL: Galileo/Gaspra](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/galileo-flyby-of-gaspra-yields-new-information/) describes the main asteroid belt around 2.2–3.3 AU and Jupiter's co-orbiting Trojans around 5.2 AU.
- [JPL: main-belt semimajor-axis distribution](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/diagrams/mb_hist.html) identifies depleted bands from the 3:1, 5:2, 7:3 and 2:1 Jupiter resonances. The generator uses approximate gap locations; widths/depletion weights are illustrative, not fitted to that histogram.
- [NASA: Sun and asteroids](https://www.nasa.gov/solar-system/how-the-sun-affects-asteroids-in-our-neighborhood/) describes Trojan groups near L4/L5, 60 degrees ahead/behind Jupiter. Our two bounded diffuse islands follow Jupiter's calculated heliocentric longitude. This is distribution context, not an N-body simulation of libration.
- [NASA: Kuiper Belt facts](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/kuiper-belt/facts/) describes the icy main region from Neptune around 30 AU to roughly 50 AU, and scattered objects with eccentric, inclined orbits extending far beyond it.

## Sampling and rendering decisions

Versioned FNV-1a/Mulberry32 streams use `orbitwatch-solar-v1:<kind>`. Quality profiles share the same prefixes: Performance 1,570 points total; Balanced 3,420; Quality 6,240. Auto resolves from reported device memory. Each population has immutable typed buffers, one non-pickable Cesium point primitive, and material uniforms for smooth opacity/density. Geometry is created lazily at system scale and reused; no particle Entities, models, React components or per-frame particle updates.

Main-belt semimajor axes span 2.2–3.3 AU, eccentricities .025–.205 and inclinations 1–24 degrees. Elliptical radial excursions are intentional. Kuiper samples contain a cold low-inclination core and a thicker illustrative component, constrained to perihelion >=30 and aphelion <=50 AU. Cool gray-blue tint distinguishes icy context from the warm, rocky belt. These parameter weights are visual sampling choices, not empirical abundance fits.

The sparse **inner scattered disk subset** has perihelia 30–38 AU, semimajor axes 52–90 AU and inclinations 10–45 degrees, reaching at most 150 AU. It fits the existing 240 AU navigation ceiling. No Oort Cloud or compressed distances are added.

Main/Kuiper/scattered distributions are static snapshots in heliocentric ecliptic axes. Their population extent remains meaningful as real destinations advance with scene time; no individual orbital motion is claimed. Trojans use a shared matrix to follow Jupiter's current longitude. Every matrix uses the same ICRF/fixed fallback as named bodies, preserving the inertial local view.

Density/opacity grows gradually with range and existing heliocentric LOD; ordinary Earth/local destination views contain no populations. GPU density ranks reveal a sparse subset before the full quality cap. Projected-area thinning reduces the compressed inner fields at overview distances, keeping their contextual points sparse. Points stay below named markers in size/brightness and are excluded from picking. Each owned primitive/material is destroyed on quality rebuild or scene cleanup. The star cube is deterministic, dark, fixed at infinity and has no camera parallax or bright nebula layer.

## Planetary rings and visual filtering

Only Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune receive procedural replacement rings. Original models/textures remain unchanged on disk, including Haumea's existing schematic ring. A shared runtime shader masks the baked giant-planet ring surfaces to avoid coincident transparent layers. Failure to prepare the replacement retains the original baked rings. The destination and moon parent alone own ring primitives; temporary departure ownership follows the existing travel lifecycle.

Radial references: [NASA Saturn ring facts](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/satringfact.html), [PDS Jupiter rings](https://pds-rings.seti.org/jupiter/jupiter_rings_table.html), [PDS Uranus rings](https://pds-rings.seti.org/uranus/uranus_rings_table.html), [PDS Uranus narrow ringlets](https://pds-rings.seti.org/uranus/uranus_tables.html), [PDS Neptune rings](https://pds-rings.seti.org/neptune/neptune_rings_table.html). Saturn's C/B/A bands retain the Cassini, Encke and Keeler gaps and a narrow F ring; the very faint D/G/E components are omitted. Jupiter has faint halo/main/gossamer subsets. Uranus includes inner ringlets, zeta and faint nu/mu bands; Neptune includes Galle, Le Verrier, Lassell and Adams. These are circular representative subsets, not complete occultation profiles or measured arc dynamics. Widths chosen within published ranges remain approximate.

Each owner uses one immutable triangle annulus buffer, bounded by 96/160/256 angular segments and 3/5/8 broad-band radial subdivisions. A seeded integer hash creates fixed object-space particulate coverage (~85% within each represented band). Pixel derivatives integrate unresolved grains to their mean, while close views reveal texture-free debris and radial structure. Narrow widths retain their radial coordinates and integrate subpixel area; they are intentionally much subtler than Saturn. Padding only provides an antialiasing raster footprint. Real band gaps are not filled to meet the visual coverage target. Opacity/colors/shadow are illustrative readability choices, not optical-depth/albedo measurements.

The ring matrix uses the exact canonical centre and current existing model orientation at each rendered timestamp, including the existing inertial local-camera cancellation. It preserves the normalized asset equatorial plane; it does not introduce an independently measured IAU pole model. No individual ring-particle orbital motion is claimed. Radius/pixel fades smoothly suppress rings before overview, without adding particle Entities, textures, timers or listeners. Quality changes and scene cleanup release geometry/materials; the shared mask is released with the layer.

Orbit strokes retain every sampled path vertex. A thin bounded core inside a padded antialiased quad replaces reciprocal glow, avoiding subpixel quad dropout and singular HDR brightness. Both native and Entity materials persist through fades. The richer 9,000-star background uses unit-sphere galactic density shared across cube seams, staying deep black/blue behind labels. Belt point brightness and size are raised within the original caps; projected-area thinning retains at least a sparse 6% overview subset rather than a solid inner ring.
