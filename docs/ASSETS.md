Other celestial surfaces retain the project's procedural artwork and are explicitly identified as illustrative. Legacy generic satellite GLBs are retained in the repository but are not exposed as verified spacecraft models.

Hubble A is copied unchanged from [NASA's source GLB](https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/cds/3d/resources/model/hubble-space-telescope-%28a%29/Hubble%20Space%20Telescope%20%28A%29.glb), SHA-256 `e5ba4de15c7d359ac8fa1ab7e286aff42dec09c0fadae3db99252587f39fa384`. The viewer only centers and uniformly scales it. Embedded materials and textures are retained; no invented spacecraft geometry is added. The asset validator checks the source hash, self-contained resources and five-primitive budget.

`prepare_bennu_reference.py` preserves NASA VTAD's compact reference geometry and normals, uniformly normalizes its volume-equivalent radius (245.887 source units) to one for the existing catalog-radius scaling, and maps the OSIRIS-REx mosaic with spherical UVs. Longitude wrap vertices are duplicated to avoid a visible interpolation seam. Its shape-to-map alignment and viewing orientation remain approximate. This is not the 3.4–17.9 million polygon OLA terrain reconstruction, and the overview mosaic does not retain the original mission's centimetre resolution. No additional displacement or small craters are fabricated. The original VTAD illustrative atlas is replaced, not presented as measured imagery.

Reproduce with `python frontend/scripts/prepare_bennu_reference.py <source.glb> <mosaic.jpg>`. Inputs: [Bennu_1_1.glb](https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/psd/solar/2023/09/b/Bennu_1_1.glb), SHA-256 `b7740b53a77ff538082d26e7079510ef7810fc3de9a5ff0fa316b393d2cf700e`; [SVS overview mosaic](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a000000/a005000/a005069/Bennu_global_FB34_FB56_ShapeV28_GndControl_MinnaertPhase30_PAN_8bit_print.jpg), SHA-256 `b321bf5a95d5ec2f48781a58a6f151fd2ea88293a8df14ccd983dd36bd666280`. Input hashes guard against silently ingesting a changed source. The legacy shape repair script skips reference geometry.

The live Sun adds an unlit, procedural photosphere shader with static spatial grain and limb darkening. This is an illustrative rendering, not a solar photograph or a live surface measurement. It uses no additional images or animated texture uploads; sub-pixel grain fades with viewing distance.

Saturn's ring mesh uses contiguous C/B/A regions and a faint Cassini Division, with approximate boundaries from the [NASA ring fact sheet](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/satringfact.html), normalized by OrbitWatch's mean-radius reference. Vertex colors keep all bands in one draw call; brightness remains schematic. Haumea's narrow ring uses the approximate 2,287 km radius and 70 km width reported by [Ortiz et al. (2017)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03113). The body shape, viewing orientation and ring opacity are illustrative; no precision pole orientation is implied.

The 17 rough procedural body meshes had discontinuities at shared seams and exaggerated independent vertex noise. `repair_reference_shapes.py` replaces that noise with continuous, gently irregular surfaces and welded vertex normals while preserving UVs and materials. Elongated bodies use illustrative axis proportions, not measured mission shape models. `check_assets.py` verifies that repaired surfaces have no open or non-manifold edges after welding coincident UV-seam vertices.

`normalize_celestial_axes.py` converts the legacy Z-up body geometry to glTF Y-up. This aligns texture poles with the generated ring planes. Its per-file marker prevents applying the rotation twice; the ring primitives are already Y-up and are preserved.

`orient_celestial_faces.py` corrects the legacy inward triangle winding of the base body meshes. Outward normals had been paired with inward-facing triangles, exposing the far hemisphere and breaking lighting. The repair changes only triangle index order, preserves all shapes/maps/rings, and is geometrically idempotent. Run it after geometry preparation; the asset validator checks orientation on every celestial body.

## Processing

`frontend/scripts/prepare_reference_assets.py` requires Python/Pillow. It bounds images, validates the 2:1 map projection, and embeds maps into existing GLBs. It does not change the underlying body geometry. `prepare_celestial_details.py` adds small ring meshes idempotently and sets the Sun material. Run the texture script before the detail script.

The previously downloaded `frontend/scripts/reference/mars-nasa.glb` uses a custom UV atlas. It is not used by the application. A copy of the rejected atlas is preserved at `frontend/scripts/reference/mars-atlas.jpg` for traceability; it must not be applied to a longitude/latitude sphere.

NASA map caveats: [JPL texture catalog](https://space.jpl.nasa.gov/tmaps/). Planet fact notes link to the relevant [NASA facts pages](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/); approximate planetary positions use the existing [JPL element model](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/planets/approx_pos.html). Small-body and moon elements remain simplified approximations, not navigation-grade ephemerides.

## Geographic overview labels
- Natural Earth 1:110m country/territory label points (177) and populated places (243), plus nonduplicated existing cities. Public domain: https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/ . Source snapshots: https://github.com/nvkelso/natural-earth-vector/tree/master/geojson . `prepare_place_labels.py` extracts only names, coordinates and rank; no boundaries are rendered or political status inferred.
- A single Cesium label collection uses altitude tiers, horizon culling and screen collision filtering at most five times per second, capped at 45 visible labels. Overview points fade out of scope below 10 km; provider-native maps supply street detail. Overlays are disabled for maps with their own labels and during celestial travel; actual fallback provider determines label behavior.
